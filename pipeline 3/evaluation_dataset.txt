HARRY POTTER AND THE SORCERER'S STONE -- RETRIEVAL/GENERATION EVALUATION DATASET
==================================================================================
Every question below was checked against the actual book text before being
included, so each answer is verifiably correct (not written from general
knowledge of the series).

Three difficulty tiers, 20 questions each (60 total):

  SET 1 - EASY:    the fact is stated directly in one short passage/scene.
                    Answers are ~1-6 words (well under 20 tokens).
  SET 2 - MEDIUM:  answering requires combining 2-3 details that appear
                    close together, within the same scene (a scene this size
                    typically spans 2-3 of our ~250-word chunks).
                    Answers are still short (under 20 tokens).
  SET 3 - HARD / DERIVED: answering requires connecting information from
                    scenes that are far apart in the book (different
                    chapters, sometimes beginning vs. end). These need
                    inference, not just lookup, so answers are longer.

No answer key/chunk-ID mapping is included here -- this is just the Q&A
ground truth. Chunk-level retrieval scoring (which chunk IDs "should" come
back for each question) is the next step, once we start pipeline 3 for real.

   A: She had broken her leg

3. Q: How old are Nicolas Flamel and his wife Perenelle?
   A: 665 and 658 years old

4. Q: How many points did Dumbledore award Ron and Hermione at the final feast, and for what?

5. Q: How many points did Neville earn at the final feast, and why?

6. Q: What was Gryffindor's final house point total, and whose total did it match?
   A: 482, tying Slytherin

SET 3 -- HARD / DERIVED (requires connecting facts from far-apart chapters)
==================================================================================

1. Q: Why is Quirrell unable to touch Harry during their final confrontation?
   A: Harry's mother died to save him, and that sacrifice left a
      protection in his skin that burns anyone marked by Voldemort's hatred.

2. Q: Why did Voldemort want the Sorcerer's Stone so badly?
   A: The Stone produces the Elixir of Life, which he needed to
      rebuild a body of his own after losing his original one.

3. Q: Why does Dumbledore say the Mirror of Erised is dangerous, based on what Harry saw in it?
   A: It shows a person's deepest desire rather than the truth or
      anything useful, and people have wasted away obsessing over it.

4. Q: How does the wand Ollivander sells Harry in Diagon Alley foreshadow the book's climax?
   A: Ollivander reveals the wand's twin gave Harry his scar, tying
      Harry and Voldemort together long before their final meeting.

5. Q: How does Hagrid's earlier slip about Fluffy end up mattering during the break-in to steal the Stone?
   A: Hagrid told a stranger (secretly Quirrell) that music puts
      Fluffy to sleep, which is exactly how the intruder gets past him.

6. Q: Why does the trio's detention in the Forbidden Forest turn out to be connected to the theft attempt on the Stone?
   A: They find a wounded unicorn and see Quirrell/Voldemort drinking
      its blood, the first solid proof someone is after the Stone.

7. Q: Why does Snape's hostility toward Harry all year turn out to be misleading?
   A: He seemed suspicious and even sabotaged Harry's Quidditch match
      in the trio's eyes, but he actually disliked Harry only because
      he had disliked Harry's father, and was secretly protecting Harry
      from Quirrell the whole time.

8. Q: How do Gryffindor's point losses and gains across the year explain their house cup win?
   A: Points lost earlier (Neville, Harry, and friends breaking rules)
      were outweighed by Dumbledore's last-minute awards for stopping
      Voldemort at the end of the year.

9. Q: Why does Quirrell wear a turban all year, and what is the payoff of that detail?
   A: It hides Voldemort's weak, reborn face living on the back of
      his head, revealed only in the final chapter.

10. Q: How do the obstacles guarding the Stone connect back to lessons taught earlier in the book?
    A: Each obstacle mirrors a class: Devil's Snare from Herbology,
       flying keys from flying lessons, giant chess from strategy, and
       a logic riddle among potion bottles from Potions.

11. Q: Why is it significant that Hermione, not Harry or Ron, solves the final potion riddle?
    A: It shows intelligence matters as much as bravery, echoed later
       when Dumbledore praises her "cool logic" at the final feast.

12. Q: How does Neville's arc across the book pay off at the end-of-year feast?
    A: After being mocked and overlooked all year, he finally stands
       up to his friends, and that courage earns Gryffindor the points
       that win them the house cup.

13. Q: Why did the trio suspect Snape of cursing Harry's broom during the Quidditch match, and how was that suspicion overturned?
    A: They saw Snape muttering and staring at Harry while his broom
       bucked wildly; later they learn Snape was actually casting a
       counter-curse to save Harry from Quirrell's jinx.

14. Q: What clue from the Quidditch match, revisited later in the book, hints that someone was protecting Harry rather than attacking him?
    A: Hermione had set Snape's robes on fire assuming he was cursing
       Harry, which actually broke his eye contact and stopped him from
       finishing the counter-curse against Quirrell.

15. Q: Why does the fact that Voldemort killed Harry's parents matter for how the final battle is resolved?
    A: Lily Potter's death to protect her son created the same
       protective magic that stops Quirrell from touching Harry years later.

16. Q: How did the trio finally connect Nicolas Flamel to what's hidden at Hogwarts, after weeks of searching?
    A: Harry happened to recall Flamel's name from a Chocolate Frog
       card, which let Hermione recognize it in a library book she'd
       already read for an unrelated reason.

17. Q: How does the recurring pain in Harry's scar connect events across the book?
    A: It flares up whenever Voldemort/Quirrell is near or feels
       strong emotion, linking the wand shop scene, the Quidditch
       match, and the final confrontation as the same ongoing threat.

18. Q: Why does Dumbledore say that Harry, Ron, and Hermione succeeded where a teacher might not have?
    A: He explains that Voldemort could not understand or anticipate
       the protection of love and the loyalty that drove the three of
       them to risk everything for each other.

19. Q: Why does the final chapter reveal that Snape had been trying to protect Harry, not harm him, all year?
    A: Quirrell explains during the confrontation that Snape had been
       working against him all along, reframing Snape's suspicious
       behavior earlier in the book as protection rather than sabotage.

20. Q: Why is Neville awarded points at the very end instead of earlier in the story, and why does it matter that it happens then?
    A: He only finds the courage to stand up to his own friends at the
       very end, and Dumbledore's timing of the award is what pushes
       Gryffindor's total just past Slytherin's to win the house cup.
